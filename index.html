    What AI actually earns inside a hyperscaler

    Azure AI gross margins, estimated from incremental Intelligent Cloud gross profit

    Reading the crossover. The integrated model earns above 80% of revenue in EBITDA but requires more than $200 of capex for every $100 of incremental revenue. Capital intensity therefore scales at roughly 2.1 times the growth rate: it crosses the EBITDA margin at about 38% growth, and everything above that line is funded externally. The conclusion is not that growth is uneconomic, but that the AI value chain needs net financing until well after 2030.

    Compute

    A second silicon market has opened.

    XPUs remain the centre of gravity, compounding at 53% to $1.7tn in 2030. But agentic workloads have opened a second market in CPUs, growing at 93% off a very low base and dragging 2030 server CPU TAM estimates from about $60bn a year ago to $200bn and above.

    Underneath both sits a single gate: TSMC allocation. Foundry capex converts to XPU revenue on a roughly two-year lag, which is what makes the supply chain's capacity plans the most reliable read on the demand curve.

    Share, and the limits of share. AMD's datacentre GPU share has stalled near 4% while its revenue has not, and its x86 server CPU share slipped a point to 44% in 2Q26 purely on supply. The house view still has AMD above 10% of XPUs and above 15% of GPUs by 2030 on the MI450 ramp.
